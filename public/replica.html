Chapter 03 · Polarin — a Network-as-a-Service platform, built at POLO

The Platform.

Three problems that shaped Polarin — told in three acts each. Scroll down; the story moves sideways, the way real roadmaps do. →

Case I · Foundation

A platform that started as a blank Figma file.

Polarin · customer portal & design system

Act I · Tension

A NaaS platform with no product surface and no design language — and I was the only designer. Every module, every flow, every screen: unmade.

Act II · Turn

Took each customer-portal module 0 → 1 — structure, core flows, screens — while building the Polarin design system (tokens, components, patterns) underneath it all.

Act III · Resolution

Every product surface at POLO still runs on that system. New modules ship faster because the language already exists.

AI in this chapterThe system became the shared vocabulary between design and dev — and later, the vocabulary Claude speaks when I generate production-adjacent frontends. A design system is a prompt library you can see.
0 → 1platform & design system
0→1 deliverydesign systemsIA & core flows
Case II · Adoption

Teaching customers to serve themselves.

Polarin · developer & customer portals

Act I · Tension

Capability lived in the platform, but usage lived in support tickets. Customers asked humans for what the portal could already do.

Act II · Turn

Rebuilt roadmap priorities around support data, usage analytics, and customer interviews — then redesigned the highest-friction flows so the obvious path was the self-serve path.

Act III · Resolution

Self-service adoption grew 3×. The portal stopped being a brochure and became the product's front door.

AI in this chapterInterview notes and support-ticket exports synthesized with Claude into friction themes I could rank. High-fidelity prototypes of the fixes built in days, tested with users before the sprint that shipped them.
3×self-service adoption
customer discoveryusage analyticsmetrics-driven
Case III · AI-native delivery

Cutting the distance between idea and production.

Polarin · frontend delivery pipeline

Act I · Tension

Frontend changes routed through outsourced vendors — slow cycles, translation loss, and specs that aged before they shipped.

Act II · Turn

Started deploying frontend changes directly via Claude and Figma in VS Code. Prototypes stopped being pictures of the spec — they became the spec, then the shipped thing.

Act III · Resolution

Outsourced vendor dependency cut ~50%; dev handoffs down 40%. The loop closed: the person who found the problem ships the fix.

AI in this chapterThis one is the AI chapter. Claude + Cursor for build, Figma & Figma Make for design-to-code, Vercel for deploys. PRD to production-adjacent frontend — one owner, days not sprints.

Chapter 04 · How I work now

The Loop.

One person, end to end — AI collapsing the distance between a question and a shipped answer. Scroll to run one full cycle; the orbit turns with you.

in the loop
01

Discover hours, not weeks

Support data, usage analytics, and customer interviews — synthesized with Claude into friction themes and jobs-to-be-done I can interrogate, rank, and challenge.

02

Define PRDs that argue back

PRDs, user stories, and OKRs drafted with AI as a sparring partner — it red-teams assumptions and pressure-tests success metrics before engineering reads a word.

03

Prototype high-fidelity, working

Not wireframes — rapid POCs on the Polarin design system with Figma, Figma Make, Cursor, and Lovable. Design instincts from Chapter 1, speed from AI pair-building.

04

Validate test the real thing

Customers click actual software in week one. Signals sharpen, feedback gets honest, and bad ideas die cheap — before they cost a sprint.

05

Deploy evidence, not opinions

Frontend changes shipped directly via Claude + Figma in VS Code, deployed on Vercel. Handoffs become head starts. Then the loop turns again.
